Decision brief
Executive readout

The weakness is not one isolated control: every area contains at least one P1 gap.

The organisation has some positive practices, including multi-factor authentication, strong passwords and a foundation for timely security updates. Those strengths are outweighed by nine high-priority gaps that expose common attack routes and make evidence of control effectiveness difficult.

01

Email and endpoint defences need immediate reinforcement.

Malware Protection is the weakest area at 35%, with no confirmed email filtering and inconsistent automatic anti-malware updates.

02

Basic visibility and account governance are incomplete.

Software inventory, administrator separation and staff-leaver controls are not yet dependable enough to demonstrate consistent operation.

03

The external attack surface is not sufficiently understood.

Default network-device credentials, unknown exposed services and missing cloud network controls create preventable exposure.

Management implication

Treat this as a short control-establishment programme, not a checklist tidy-up.

The priority is to create accountable, repeatable controls and retain evidence that they operate. Starting an application before closing the P1 gaps is likely to create avoidable rework.

Decision required

Assign a named business sponsor and technical owner for a 30-day P1 remediation sprint.

Existing strengths to preserve

Multi-factor authentication • strong passwords • prompt patching foundation • clear update responsibilities

Prototype recommendations derived from the supplied findings

Sequence work to close application blockers first, then build repeatable operating evidence.

Now / 0-30 days

Close the nine P1 gaps

  • Secure network-device credentials, exposed services and cloud boundaries.
  • Establish software visibility and separate administrator access.
  • Implement leaver controls, email filtering and automatic anti-malware updates.
  • Confirm every operating system is supported.
Recommended owner
Business sponsor + IT lead/MSP

Evidence
Configuration exports, inventories, account records and control screenshots
Next / 31-60 days

Complete the P2 control layer

  • Complete internet-connected device inventory and firewall-log review.
  • Standardise secure configuration and device-lock settings.
  • Apply least privilege, remove shared accounts and review user access.
  • Add web filtering, awareness training and firmware governance.
Recommended owner
IT lead/MSP + People/operations

Evidence
Review logs, build standard, training record and firmware register
Then / 61-90 days

Validate and prepare

  • Restrict mobile app sources where appropriate.
  • Document testing for critical security patches.
  • Improve staff update communications.
  • Run a final internal evidence review before application.
Recommended owner
Business sponsor + technical owner

Evidence
Completed readiness checklist and approved exception record

Management implication

This plan needs named internal ownership even if an MSP performs the technical work. Recommended owners and timeframes must be confirmed with the organisation before being treated as commitments.

Email and endpoint defences need immediate reinforcement.

35 / Needs work
Management implication

Common attack routes remain insufficiently controlled.

The current answers indicate that malicious email, unsafe browsing and inconsistent endpoint protection could bypass otherwise positive password and access practices. Leadership should treat filtering and automatic protection updates as immediate control-establishment work.

P1

No confirmed email filtering

Malicious attachments and phishing links are not consistently detected or blocked before reaching users.

P1

Anti-malware updates are inconsistent

Some devices may not receive current protection automatically, leaving known threats insufficiently controlled.

P2

No confirmed web filtering

Users may reach known malicious sites without a preventative browser or network control.

P2

Awareness training is inconsistent

Staff may not reliably recognise phishing, suspicious downloads or other common malware routes.

P3

Mobile app-source restrictions are uncertain

Business-used mobile devices may allow software from unapproved sources.

Prototype action recommendation

Establish layered email, web and endpoint protection, then retain operating evidence.

TimeframeP1 controls in 0-30 days; P2/P3 in 31-90 days

Evidence & method
Preparation guidance
Evidence checklist

The final readiness review should test whether controls operate, not merely whether policies exist.

Required for the first review

  • Network-device credential-change record
  • Approved external-service and cloud-rule register
  • Current software and supported-OS inventory
  • Privileged-account list and leaver procedure
  • Email-filter and endpoint-protection configuration
  • Evidence that automatic anti-malware updates are operating

Supporting operating evidence

  • Firewall-log review record
  • Secure device-build standard and sample checklist
  • User-access review and account-removal evidence
  • Web-filter configuration and staff-training record
  • Network firmware inventory and review date
  • Patch-testing and security-update communication records

Assessment basis

This prototype uses the scores, summaries and 24 findings contained in the supplied BrightCert PDF dated 26 July 2026. Recommended owners, timeframes, evidence and management wording are design-prototype enhancements and must be confirmed by the organisation.

Limitations

The assessment is based on self-reported answers. BrightCert has not performed a technical audit, vulnerability scan or official certification assessment. This checklist is preparation guidance, not an official Certification Body evidence request.

Official next step

After the gaps are addressed and evidence has been internally reviewed, apply for official Cyber Essentials through an IASME-licensed Certification Body.
